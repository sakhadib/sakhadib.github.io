I study what large language models can and cannot do in the places mainstream benchmarks skip: low-resource languages such as Bangla, figurative and legal text, and the political and safety behaviour of models once the language or the framing changes. I try to build datasets and evaluation protocols that could falsify a claim about model capability, not just confirm it.

My first-author paper on ideological plasticity in LLMs received the Best Paper Award at AACL-IJCNLP 2026. It argues that a model's political position is better described as a shape over contexts than as a single point.

Research

01

Multilingual & low-resource NLP

Bangla resources and benchmarks: a 10,361-idiom corpus, a corpus of legal acts from 1799 to 2025, and why models read idioms literally and retrieve unevenly across languages.

02

LLM evaluation

Evaluation built to test assumptions: what benchmarks actually measure, consistency in temporal reasoning, and game-based protocols for multi-turn behaviour.

03

Safety in low-resource languages

The gap between a model's ability to understand harmful content and its ability to contain it, in Bangla derogatory speech and adversarial dialogue.

04

LLMs & social science

Measuring the political behaviour of language models with instruments from political science and psychometrics, treating ideology as a context-conditioned distribution.
